Valet Parking for Weddings in Delhi NCR: A Complete Planning Guide

Parking is the first and last thing your guests experience. Here's how to plan smooth valet parking for a wedding at a farmhouse, banquet or hotel.

By the Let's Drive team · 10 November 2026

Weddings in Delhi NCR often mean hundreds of guests arriving within the same hour – at farmhouses on MG Road and Chhatarpur, banquet halls in Gurgaon, or hotels near the airport. Without a plan, cars queue on the road, guests walk long distances in formal wear, and the evening starts with stress. Professional valet parking fixes this.

How many valets do you need?

It depends on three things: how many cars you expect, how many arrive in the busiest hour, and how far the parking area is from the entrance. The further the parking, the longer each valet takes per car.

As a rough starting point, plan one valet for every 15–20 cars expected in the peak hour, plus a supervisor and someone managing keys. Share your guest list size and venue layout with your valet partner and let them confirm the final team.

Venue checklist

  • Parking capacity: how many cars fit, and is there overflow space nearby?
  • Drop-off point: a clear, well-lit spot near the entrance where cars can stop safely.
  • Distance: how long it takes to walk from the parking area back to the drop-off.
  • Key management: a secure key stand and a token or slip for every car.
  • Signage and lighting for the valet stand and the parking area.
  • Timing: valets should arrive before the first guests and stay until the last car leaves.
  • Coordination with venue security and traffic marshals.

Questions to ask a valet parking company

  • Are your valets verified and trained in key handling?
  • Will the team be in uniform, and who supervises on the day?
  • How do you track keys and vehicles?
  • What happens if a car is damaged – what is your policy?
  • Can you add valets at short notice if more guests arrive?

Tips for a smooth evening

  • Mention valet parking on the invitation or the guests' WhatsApp group.
  • Arrange a separate, quick lane for elderly guests and VIPs.
  • Plan for the "exit rush" at the end of the night – that's when valets are busiest.
  • For smaller house parties, an on-demand valet may be enough.
